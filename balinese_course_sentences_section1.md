# Balinese App Sentence Curriculum — Section 1: The Basics (A1)

> **Companion file to:** `balinese_course_vocabulary.md`
> **Format:** `Balinese sentence -> English translation`
>
> **Construction rules used in this file:**
> 1. Every vocabulary entry of a lesson appears in **at least 3 sentences** within that lesson.
> 2. Each sentence introduces **at most ONE new vocabulary entry**; every other word is drawn from the current or previous lessons (cumulative progression).
> 3. Sentences follow **register consistency** where practical: *Alus* (refined) words pair with *Alus/Mider* words; *Andap* (familiar) words pair with *Andap/Mider* words. *Mider* (neutral) words pair with both.
> 4. Within a lesson, the teaching order may differ slightly from the vocabulary list order so that the "one new entry per sentence" rule can be satisfied (e.g. `Bali`/`Jawa` are taught before `saking`/`uli` in Lesson 4).
> 5. Entries that are multi-word phrases (e.g. `sapunapi gatrane`, `nunas tulung`) count as **one** vocabulary entry.
> 6. Balinese proper names (Wayan, Made, Nyoman, Ketut, Putu, Kadek, etc.) are used as placeholders for people's names. They are **not** counted as vocabulary entries (the same way language apps use names like "Ana" or "Tom").
> 7. Grammatical particles are not vocabulary entries and may appear freely: the definite suffix **-ne / -né** ("the / his / her"), the question particle **-a?** (yes/no questions), and the topic marker **ba**.

### Lesson 1: Basics 1

**Focus:** pronouns, yes/no, correct/incorrect, this/that, here/there, to be (exist), and/but.
**Available grammar:** Balinese has no separate verb "to be" in the present — `Niki patut.` literally means "This correct." Existence/location uses `wénten` (Alus) / `ada` (Andap).

### Lesson 2: Greetings

## Section 1 Completion Summary

After Lesson 10, the learner can:

- **Identify & point:** say who/what is here or there using pronouns, `niki/ene/nika/ento`, `dini/driki/ditu/drika`, and `ada/wénten`.
- **Greet & respond:** `Om Swastiastu`, time-of-day greetings with `rahajeng`, ask and answer `sapunapi gatrane?` / `kenken kabare?`, take leave with `pamit/mepamit`, and bless with `dumogi rahayu`.
- **Be polite:** thank (`suksma/suksma/matur suksma`), reply (`suksma mewali/ten wénten/sing ada`), apologize (`ampura/nunas ampura/ampurayang/pelih/iwang`), ask for help (`nunas tulung/ngidih tulung`), and invite (`durus/raris/silakan`, `jalan/ngiring`).
- **Introduce:** give and ask names in all three politeness levels, say where they are from and where they stay, and talk about learning `Basa Bali`.
- **Eat & drink:** order and describe food and drinks, say they are hungry/thirsty, and name the meals of the day.
- **Talk about animals:** name 16+ animals in both registers.
- **Express possession:** say what is mine/yours/his with `gelah/druwe(n)`, ask `napi niki?`/`apa ene?`, bring (`ngaba/makta`), give (`baang/wehin`), borrow (`silih/nyelang`), and ask permission (`dadi/dados`).
- **Count & shop basics:** numbers 1–10, `kuda/kude/pinten`, `aji kuda?`/`kude regane?`, quantities (`liu/akeh`, `bedik/kidik`, `abedik/akidik`), and `pragat/puput/telah`.
- **Talk about family:** the full immediate family plus grandparents, cousins, and in-laws in both registers.
- **Ask anything:** the complete basic question-word system (`dija`, `kija`, `pidan`, `nyen/sira`, `apa/napi`, `kenken/sapunapi`, `kenapa`, `kuda/pinten`, `encen`) plus wants (`dot/meled/kayun`) and willingness (`nyak/arsa`).

**Ready for Section 2,** where verbs of routine, places, directions, time, adjectives, and feelings will unlock full sentence building.
